# Free Speech & Moderation

BlockTrust is built on a free-speech commitment that is unusual among social
platforms today: the project does not censor lawful, non-hateful expression.
This page explains the policy in plain language, how it is enforced, and why it
is central to the project's identity. It expands on the relevant sections of
`GOVERNANCE.md` and `CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md` and is the reference for anyone —
user, contributor, or instance operator — who wants to understand where the
lines are drawn.

---

## The core principle

The rule is simple and deliberately narrow. The only content BlockTrust removes
or restricts is:

1. **Hate speech** — content that attacks or dehumanises people on the basis of
   protected characteristics (race, ethnicity, national origin, religion, sex,
   gender identity, sexual orientation, disability, or age).
2. **Illegal content** — content that is unlawful in the jurisdiction where it
   is posted, including but not limited to genuine threats of violence, child
   sexual abuse material, non-consensual intimate imagery, and content
   facilitating serious crime.
3. **Spam** — automated or repetitive content that exists to manipulate,
   overload, or advertise without genuine community participation.

Everything else is protected, even when it is unpopular, controversial,
offensive to some, or simply wrong. Disagreement, criticism, uncomfortable
opinions, and minority viewpoints are not violations of this policy. They are
the reason the policy exists.

---

## Why this stance

Most incumbent social platforms have expansive content policies that police
tone, topic, and even the framing of ideas, enforced by opaque algorithms and
unaccountable moderation teams. The result is that lawful, non-hateful speech is
routinely suppressed, particularly speech that is unpopular or outside the
current consensus. BlockTrust takes the opposite position because a
neighbourhood platform that cannot host an honest conversation is not a
neighbourhood platform — it is a PR channel.

The free-speech stance is not a cover for the three excluded categories. Hate
speech, illegal content, and spam are removed precisely because they prevent the
honest conversation the platform is for. The line is drawn at *attack on
people* and *genuine harm*, not at *discomfort with ideas*.

---

## Behaviour vs. ideas

The Code of Conduct distinguishes attacking *ideas* from attacking *people*.
This is the load-bearing distinction of the whole policy.

- **Attacking ideas is welcome.** "That proposal is wrong, and here is why" is
  always fine, however forcefully expressed.
- **Attacking people is not.** "You are stupid for proposing that" crosses the
  line into personal attack, which is a Code of Conduct matter regardless of
  the idea being discussed.

Moderation focuses on conduct toward people, not on the substance of opinions.
This keeps the space open to a wide range of views while keeping it civil enough
to be usable.

---

## How moderation works in the project's own spaces

In BlockTrust's GitHub spaces (issues, pull requests, Discussions, the wiki),
maintainers enforce the Code of Conduct as described in `GOVERNANCE.md`. The
escalation path is: private correction → warning → temporary ban → permanent
ban, with the severity matched to the behaviour. Moderation actions are
documented (without exposing private details of the people involved) so the
community can see that the policy is applied consistently and not as a tool for
silencing dissent.

A maintainer who attempts to censor lawful, non-hateful speech outside the three
excluded categories is acting against the project's mission. Such actions can
and should be challenged through governance, and persistent misuse of moderation
to suppress protected speech is grounds for removing a maintainer.

---

## How moderation works for instance operators

The software BlockTrust ships is a front-end; when a backend is introduced per
the Roadmap, operators running their own instances will make their own
moderation calls. The project's default policy — remove only the three
categories above — is the recommended baseline, and the governance commitments
(minimise data, support deletion, never sell data) apply to any official
instance. Operators are free to adopt stricter rules for their own communities,
but the project will not build censorship tooling that goes beyond the baseline
into the main codebase. Communities that want heavier moderation can fork.

---

## Reporting and appeals

If you believe content in a project space violates the policy, report it to the
maintainers per `SUPPORT.md`. If you believe a moderation action was wrong —
including that it suppressed protected speech — there is an appeals path: raise
it in a Discussion, or, if that is not safe, contact the Lead Maintainer
privately. Appeals of moderation are taken seriously precisely because
over-moderation is the failure mode the project is designed to resist.

---

## What this policy is not

- It is not a promise of a platform for everything. The three categories are
  excluded, and the Code of Conduct governs behaviour.
- It is not "anything goes." Harassment, doxxing, and threats are not protected
  speech under this policy; they are violations.
- It is not a legal document. For the formal terms, see the Code of Conduct and
  Governance documents. This page is the plain-language explanation.
- It is not static. If experience shows the policy needs clarification, it is
  updated through the governance process in the open, with the reasoning
  recorded.

The aim is a neighbourhood platform that treats its users as adults capable of
handling disagreement. That is the block BlockTrust is trying to build.
